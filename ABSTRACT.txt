Generative models produce adult portraits whose global appearance is often convincing while facial skin still reads as wax, airbrushed polymer, or a repeated texture stamp. Distributional image scores, biometric face-image quality, and forensic detector outputs answer different questions, and a smooth cheek can be either a photographic fact or a synthesis failure. This paper specifies the plastic-skin protocol, version 1, for still, photorealistic portraits of adults. The contribution is an operational definition, a seven-family taxonomy grounded in measured skin optics, ordinal severity anchors, a pre-registerable human protocol with sampling strata, negative controls, blinding, and agreement reporting, and a short list of optional instrumental covariates that are explicitly unvalidated. A qualitative walkthrough applies the instrument to three constructed cases. No generator is ranked. The paper reports no new quantitative experiment, no detector accuracy, and no rater-study sample size. A reporting checklist is included so that later studies can be compared without collapsing the phenomenon to a single leaderboard number.
